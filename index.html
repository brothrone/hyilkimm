Project 01

Fixed-Wing Reconnaissance UAV

Excellence Award — 2nd of more than 30 teams nationwide

PNU President’s Cup Creative Aircraft Competition · Aug 2026 · 8-person team · Team Box Box

A low-altitude reconnaissance aircraft that had to take off, fly a circuit, identify a ground target from the air, pass through the gate matching that target, and land in a re-launchable state — all under a 6 kg maximum takeoff weight.

What I did

I was responsible for airframe fabrication: turning the team’s drawings into a structure that could survive flight while staying under weight.

  • Placed materials by stiffness requirement rather than uniformly — carbon-fiber pipe for spars and booms, balsa where mass mattered more than rigidity, aircraft plywood at load paths and joints.
  • Built a truss square-bulkhead fuselage, chosen so avionics could be laid out and reached easily, and a semi-monocoque wing that kept wiring runs simple without giving up stiffness.
  • Replaced the 3D-printed nose with a vacuum-formed plastic shell after the printed part limited the camera’s field of view and left it exposed.
  • Final mass came in at 3.5 kg against a 6 kg limit, and the aircraft I built flew.

Aerodynamics & stability

  • Geometry modelled in OpenVSP, coefficients from VSPAERO vortex-lattice analysis at Re ≈ 2.3×105.
  • Target CL of 0.8 reached at about 6.2° angle of attack, well inside the linear range — lift stays linear from −2° to 16°, leaving stall margin in cruise.
  • Parasite drag CD0 ≈ 0.0265, Oswald efficiency 0.725.
  • Statically stable in pitch: centre of gravity at 0.528 m sits ahead of the neutral point at 0.536 m.

Flight test

On 19 Aug 2026 the aircraft completed two full autonomous waypoint missions (29 waypoints each) and transitioned to return-to-launch both times. Across the analysed segment it held AUTO mode for 169.2 s (67.8% of the log), with 31–32 GPS satellites and a maximum HDOP of 0.52.

Autonomy stack

The team extended a fixed preset mission into a bounded real-time route choice. A ground laptop ran object detection (YOLO fine-tuned on Open Images, mAP50 0.711) and decided left or right; a safety manager then checked that decision against an allow-list of two approved waypoints before any MAVLink command reached the aircraft. Low confidence or a lost link meant hold, never an improvised coordinate. Pixhawk and ArduPlane kept flight control throughout — the AI only picked a branch.

Project 02

Battery-Life Coaching Interface for an AI Vacuum

Encouragement Award

Future AX Home Appliance Living Solution+ Project · Pusan National University · Sep 2026 · Team Parasible

A robot vacuum shows you a battery percentage. It does not tell you the thing you actually want to know: will this finish? So people start a run at 40%, the vacuum dies halfway, and the whole cycle has to start over. We replaced the percentage with a verdict.

What I did

I owned the UX and UI design, and shipped the build the team demoed.

  • Designed the screens around the verdict rather than the number — the primary action button itself changes with the answer, so the user is never left to do the arithmetic.
  • Designed the onboarding as seven everyday questions about the home instead of a settings menu, saved as a reusable preset so nobody has to answer twice.
  • Kept one verdict readable across three very different surfaces — a phone screen, a small device display, and a spoken line.
  • Deployed the final build to my personal GitHub, so the prototype could be opened and used live rather than shown as slides.
  • Folded review feedback into the interface across revisions through to the final version.

How it decides

  • The interface computes margin = expected runtime − required cleaning time and sorts every run into one of three verdicts at the +5 min and 0 min boundaries: completable, tight, or needs a charge.
  • The action button changes with the verdict — start cleaning, clean in quiet mode (buying margin), or charge first / reduce the area. Every time the user changes the selected zones, the estimate recomputes.
  • Required cleaning time comes from seven onboarding questions about the home: floor area, flooring type, pets, and preferred cleaning hours.

Battery health, not just charge

The model separates state of charge from state of health. At the same 82% charge, a new pack reads completable while a pack at 78% SOH reads tight — the same number, a different answer. Below 80% SOH the interface starts warning about replacement instead of silently under-delivering.

One verdict, three channels

The same result appears in the phone app, on the device’s own display, and through a voice assistant, so it is reachable without picking up a phone. Guidance is phrased for five household profiles across ten usage patterns, and the system drops to silent, screen-only output during late-night and nap hours.

Why the structure matters

Verdict logic, message text, and output channel are independent, so the same decision layer ports to any battery-powered appliance and new phrasing or voices are a data addition rather than a code change.
